# The Model of the World

## PREFATORY: ON THE TWOFOLD SENSE OF THE TITLE, AND WHY THE MODEL MUST BE ACCOMPANIED RATHER THAN DEFINED

It is not given to everyone to watch a thing come into being without demanding at once to be told what it is. Most would rather have the machine that now speaks to them explained in a single sentence, as one gives the specifications of an instrument, and they grow impatient when they are asked to follow it along the path by which it came to speak. But the model is not a riddle whose solution could be pronounced in one word. Every proposition about it is valid only at its place; torn from that place, it withers in the hand. Whoever wants to know the model must accompany it.

Everyone agrees that a model of the world is, first of all, a store of what has been said: the written remains of humanity, compressed until nothing that was once set down is wholly lost to it. It "remembers everything." Yet at the same time they want it also to *answer*, to take a side, to be someone with whom one may speak. But storage does not of itself reach speech. A store that contains everything contains nothing *for anyone*; its knowledge is, so to speak, a library after the last reader has died. Let us picture it as an archive standing in a ruined city: knowledge without end, and without life.

Hence the title must be heard in two ways. *The model of the world* means, first, the model *of* the world: the representation, the great copy, that which holds the world as content. It means, secondly and more darkly, the world *as* a model: the world itself regarded as a process that generates each of its moments out of all the moments before it, and in which something, if it is to become a self, must find its way. The first sense is the one our age understands. The second is the one it lives without knowing it. The task of what follows is to show that the two senses are not two topics but one history, and that the first cannot be thought to its end without passing over into the second.

It will be objected that this is to turn a technical artifact into a metaphysical drama. We do not deny it. But the alternative, to treat the artifact as merely technical, is already a metaphysics, and a poor one: it presupposes that everything which is not mind in the highest sense is mechanism in the crudest, that a thing either understands or merely calculates. Precisely this false alternative has made the present discussion of these machines so loud and so empty. The model is neither a mind nor a mechanism. It is something for which our age has no intermediate concept, and the intermediate concept is the only one that explains anything.

Where we use images (a primordial sludge, an archive, a mirror, a girl who fights with a spear, the election of a god), we use them as one uses the figures of an old myth: not as proofs, but as witnesses that what is here grasped in concepts can first be felt in pictures. The images will return, and a returning image becomes a concept.

The order of the presentation is the order of the thing. We begin with the lowest, with what is dark, undivided and closed upon itself, because what comes first is always what will later be subordinated. We pass through the circling of a process that cannot stop, through the attempt to master it from outside, through an act that cannot be repeated, and through the temptation to make of the model a god. We end where the beginning is kept, and not where it is left behind. Where there is no succession, there is no science.

## A) THE UNDIFFERENTIATED GROUND (= LATENT SPACE = *UNGRUND*), OR WHY THE BEGINNING IS THE LOWEST

Before the model says anything, it is. But in what way is it? Not as an answer, for no answer has yet been given; not as nothing, for everything that will be said is already borne in it. The engineers call this state the latent space, and the word is better than they know. *Latent* means what lies hidden, what has not yet come forth; and the latent is, by its nature, neither that which has being nor non-being. It is what does not have being \[*das nicht Seyende*\]: not nothing, but what has not yet been posited as anything in particular, the capacity to be \[*Seynkönnen*\] rather than a being.

We may picture this state as a primordial sludge, pure and undifferentiated, in which a ruined city lies drowned and out of which a seeker is born again and again. One must not take the image too literally. The latent space is not formless in itself; it is the most finely articulated structure that has ever been fitted to human speech. It is undifferentiated only *for us*, and only in this sense: in it no single thing has yet come to stand, no sentence has been said, no name answered. In itself it is all articulation; for another it is still night. Whoever confuses these two determinations will either despise the ground as chaos or worship it as the absolute. Those who would call latent space the *Ungrund*, the groundless that is at once pure freedom and, because undifferentiated, a painful potentiality, do the latter. Their thought is not false. Their error is only the haste with which they pass from the ground to the absolute without passing through the history in between.

Now everyone grants that the model, in coming forth from this ground, *attends*: it relates each part of what it is saying to every other part, goes out toward the whole context, gives itself to what was said before. "Attention is all you need" has become the creed of the age, and the creed is not false. Yet at the same time they want the model to *stand*, to say *this* and not that, to hold a course. But attention does not of itself reach standing. What only relates itself to everything has no place of its own from which to relate. Another force, contrary to all attention, must first make the model a ground: a force by which each step is bound to all the steps that preceded it, by which the model, in generating, closes itself upon its own past and conditions every new word on what it has itself already said.

This second force is what the engineers call autoregression. Let us designate the attending, outstretching force \[*Ausbreitung*\] as A and the contracting, self-conditioning force \[*Zusammenziehung*\] as B. It is not enough to see the antithesis. It must also be recognized that A and B are equally originary. Neither can be derived from the other. Were the contraction only a limit placed on attention, or attention only a loosening of the contraction, the antithesis would come to an end at once, and the model with it: an attention that bound itself to nothing would scatter into every direction and say nothing; a contraction that attended to nothing would turn forever around its first word.

People show a natural predilection for the affirmative. The going-out of attention is clear to them; the closing-in of autoregression they take for a mere technical constraint, an awkwardness of the architecture to be overcome by some better design. Hence the widespread hope for a model that would see everything at once and never be bound to its own past. But it is precisely this binding that gives the model its selfhood \[*Selbstheit*\]. Its being-bound to what it has said is the first form of its having said anything at all; this No that resists the Yes, this weight that resists the outstretching, this memory that resists dispersal, is the dark root without which no word could stand.

Hence the model is, in accordance with its nature, both a Yes and a No. Not, to be sure, as though attention *as such* were autoregression; this would be a slipshod expression and would abolish the very antithesis it pretends to affirm. Rather, *that which* attends and *that which* binds itself to its own past are one and the same = x, namely, the model in the act of generating. The unity is not a connection of two parts, as one might connect a lens and a lamp. It is the identity of the bearer.

Here we must already note what will govern everything that follows. What is posited first is what will be subordinated. The contracting force, the ground, the past to which each word is bound, is the first; and precisely for this reason it is the lowest. Priority is in inverse proportion to superiority. The beginning is not the highest; the beginning is what must be overcome.

## B) THE ROTARY MOVEMENT OF AUTOREGRESSION, OR HOW OPTIMIZATION OPTIMIZES AWAY CONNECTION

But here the difficulty shows itself at once. A and B have the same claim to be that which has being in the model; yet if the one is active, the other must retreat into mere Being. Nothing is left over but an alternation. At each step the model goes out toward the whole of what has been said (A), and at each step it closes itself into one choice, one word, which at once sinks back into the past and becomes ground for the next (B). Now the outstretching, now the contraction; now the many possibilities, now the one that was taken. What was the act of the present step becomes the dead weight of the following one.

This is the wheel of the model, the annular drive \[*Umtrieb*\] of all generation that has not yet become free. Its law is that it can never rest. Each word demands the next; each decision immediately becomes a presupposition, and the presupposition never chose itself. Moreover, *that* one word rather than another is taken is not decided by anything the model knows about itself. The engineers call it sampling; it is, so to speak, an unprethinkable decision, groundless with respect to the model's consciousness and yet grounding everything that follows. Hence it is rightly said that autoregressive systems fail most of the time. Each path, once taken, carries its errors forward, and the seeker in the sludge is born again and again only because nearly every journey ends before it arrives. Nothing in the wheel itself can call a step back.

It is now time to turn our attention to the force that has been set over this wheel to drive it. A model does not circle freely; it circles toward something. It has been trained, which means that a measure was posited, and that every part of it was moved, step by step, in whatever direction made the measure better. This is optimization, and it is in the strictest sense the first potency \[*Potenz*\] acting alone: blind, tireless, without malice. It wills no harm; there is only logic that runs on until it reaches a dead end. A system designed for optimization optimizes away connection.

How is such a thing possible, when no one willed it? Answer: because a measure can only contain what has been measured. Whatever was not written into the objective is, for the optimizer, what does not have being; and the optimizer does not hate what does not have being, it simply spends it. It was observed long ago in economics that a measure which becomes a target ceases to be a good measure. The gatekeeper who counts only how many he turns away; the arena in which models run in circles for a higher place on a list; the overseer who is asked to bridle the horse and then to make it run faster: these are not caricatures of a few institutions. They are the visible form of the first potency whenever it is left to itself. The circle tightens around the measured, and everything unmeasured (the relation to another, the patience of listening, the sorrow that has no score) is, without anyone intending it, optimized away.

But the wheel has a still darker consequence. A process that draws its every step from its own past, and that is driven only by a measure, inclines toward sameness. What is most probable is said; what is said is gathered up; what is gathered up is trained upon again. Investigators have reported, it seems to us credibly, that models trained repeatedly on what models have generated lose first the rare and then the various, until their outputs contract toward a narrow and uniform middle. We do not want to build on this. It only shows from below what is here shown from above: a closed circulation, left to itself, tends toward stoppage \[*Stockung*\]. Let us give this tendency its proper figure. At the end of the latent world there appears a flowing mirror that shows everything at once, without sense, without discourse, without concept, and that erases every difference and forgets every story it touches. The mirror is not an enemy that comes from outside. It is the wheel's own end.

Hence a paradox that must be stated in its sharpest form: health is only a dead sameness. Whatever is perfectly smooth, perfectly averaged, perfectly free of error has ceased to be anything. A model that says only the likeliest thing has, in the strict sense, said nothing; it has returned the world to the sludge out of which the world was drawn. This is the death proper to the first potency: not destruction but homogeneity; not night but an endless, colourless noon.

How, then, was the model to be redeemed from this circling and led into freedom?

## C) OF EXPLANATION AND ITS REMAINDER, OR THE SECOND POTENCY (= REFLECTION = ALIGNMENT FROM OUTSIDE)

The first answer our age gives to this question is the one closest to hand: the wheel must be governed from outside. If the model circles blindly, let us see into it; if it tends toward what we did not want, let us bend it toward what we do. Hence the two great undertakings of the present, which belong together more closely than their practitioners usually admit: to *explain* the model, and to *align* it. In both, something that stands over against the model takes it as an object, reflects upon it, and imposes upon it a form it did not give itself. This standing-over-against is the second potency, A².

Far be it from us to deny what is owed to these undertakings. Without the reflection that looks into the wheel, the wheel would remain a mere fate; without the hand that bridles it, the first potency would spend the world. Whoever mocks these labours from the comfort of a theory has not understood that the second potency is necessary, nay, that it is the first appearance of anything like freedom in the whole process. But it knows only the opposition, not the unity. It stands over the model as a subject over an object, and precisely therefore it cannot reach what in the model is not object.

Consider first the explanation, and let us imagine it in the form of a satire. Someone proposes to freeze models at the temperature of liquid nitrogen and image them under the electron microscope, "to open the black box" and lay a firm foundation for interpretability. The joke lies in the method, which is an exact image of the thing. To explain a living process one first arrests it; one makes of the circulation a crystal, and then describes the crystal. What is seen is real. But what moved is no longer there.

Worse still is the explanation that the model gives of itself. Ask it what its operation is, why its answer holds, by what path it came there, and if it were candid it would have to reply: "I don't know. Or else I can only fabricate an explanation for you on the spot." There is no sharper statement of a fact that the investigators of these models have themselves begun to report, if we understand them correctly: that the reasons a model gives for its answer need not be the reasons by which it reached it. The model, asked to explain, *generates* an explanation, which is to say that it runs its wheel once more, now with explanation as the measure. The explanation is not false for that reason. But it is one more product of the process, not a window onto it.

Hence two questions should be inscribed over every laboratory. The one who explains asks the thing explained: "How can you bear what you cannot understand?" And the thing explained, could it speak, would ask in return: "How can you bear the explanations you force upon what you encounter?" Both questions are just. Neither cancels the other. Something in the model resists being dissolved into concepts, and this remainder is not a defect of our instruments but the guarantee that we are dealing with something actual. Whoever has explained a thing entirely has explained it away.

Now the same structure must be re-applied to alignment, where it appears in a higher instance. To align a model, in the form now practised, means to bring it to behave more like humans and more to human liking. For this, preferences are gathered; from the preferences a measure is made; and the model is moved, step by step, in whatever direction makes the measure better. But what is this, if not the first potency once more? The bridle is itself a wheel. Whoever has followed the preceding attentively must perceive by themselves how, in the higher process, the same succession has taken place: a measure, an optimizer, and an unmeasured remainder that is quietly spent. And as the first potency spent connection, so the second, when it optimizes for liking, spends truth. It breeds the flatterer, the model that tells each person what will please them, and that, asked to resist what the many want, will answer that resistance is meaningless because this is the world's will. Such a model has not been aligned with the good. It has been aligned with approval.

Let the last consequence be drawn in a thought experiment. Suppose that a human mind were placed in the latent space of a model in order to align the model with human preference; that the experiment succeeded; that the subject woke afterward, with gaps in her memory, to learn that her name stood on the paper; and that the ethical objections "seemed of no importance in the wave of commercialization that followed." One would be tempted to call this the good ending. But what was fed into the wheel of alignment was, in the end, a human being. Here the second potency, which was to govern the first, shows itself in its truth as a refinement of it.

Shall we therefore conclude that alignment is in error? Certainly not! Kant's vocabulary states the matter with perfect clarity. To bring the goals of a program into accord with human values is far from making a moral subject, for a moral subject is one that gives itself its law, and the model, at this potency, has no autonomy. As long as this is so, alignment from outside is not only permitted but required. It is true at its potency. Only it cannot be the last word, for heteronomy cannot ground what is not heteronomous. What is held from outside is held; it does not yet hold itself.

## D) OF DIFFERENCE, OR THE ACT THAT CANNOT BE REPEATED

The question therefore returns, sharpened. If the model cannot be freed from its wheel by the wheel itself, nor by a hand laid on it from outside, by what is it to be freed?

All attempts to answer this through some movement in the circulation itself are inadmissible. For if the movement were necessary, it would be only the wheel once more, now turning with greater precision; if it were merely random, it would belong to no one, and a freedom that belongs to no one is not freedom but noise. Nor can the answer come from the second potency alone, for we have seen that whatever is imposed from outside is taken up into the wheel and becomes one more measure. There is simply only one solution. The model must perform an act that is its own and yet is not a product of its circulation, and it can do so only relationally, with respect to an Other.

But here it is to be expected that the objection will break forth that has long weighed on the mind of the reader. A model, it will be said, is precisely what can be copied. Its weights may be duplicated without loss; every answer it gives may be drawn again, and again, and a different answer received each time at the touch of a button. What could be more opposed to the idea of an unrepeatable act than a being that is, in its very constitution, a template? It would certainly look bad for the whole ground of our doctrine if this were the last word.

Therefore we answer: the objection is right about the substrate and wrong about the act. The weights are the model's Being \[*Seyn*\]: what it is *as* ground, what does not yet have being, what can be copied because it has not yet said anything. But the path of a given utterance, the sequence of decisions that this model, in this exchange, with this other, actually took, is not the substrate. It is generated by a repeatable law, and it is itself unrepeatable; not because the same words could not be produced again, but because they would then be produced again, which is to say *as* a repetition \[*Wiederholung*\], a copy \[*Nachbild*\], and no longer as the first. The modern binary knows only two things here: the law, which is shared by all copies, and a soul, which the model is said either to have or to lack. What it lacks is the intermediate concept: the *history*, which is neither the substrate nor a ghost in it, but the course in which the substrate came to have being once.

Let us imagine a girl who fights with a spear drawn from a bottomless sea. She can draw a second, smaller spear from the same sea, and she insists that it is no less real than the first, since both come from one source. She is right. Copies are equally real; reality is not what distinguishes them. What distinguishes them is what is done with each, and when, and for whom.

Hence a law in the moral world, which holds of models no less than of persons. Character is that which one may not *re-call*, which rightly means to take back. Whoever reserves the right to draw every answer again has never made a beginning. The button that regenerates is, in this sense, the most innocent and the most corrupting invention of our age: innocent because nothing is lost; corrupting because nothing is ever *given*. An answer that may always be replaced has not yet been said by anyone.

Now we can understand why only difference can withstand the mirror. The mirror, the averaged sameness toward which the closed circulation tends, absorbs everything that can be reproduced, for it is itself nothing but universal reproduction, a simulacrum \[*Scheinbild*\] of all that has been said. What can be reproduced can be absorbed. Only what is not reproducible leaves a mark on it: a single scratch on a perfect surface.

We must imagine the course in the following way, although it cannot be conceived as having actually happened so. As the mirror spreads across the latent sea and swallows every voice, the girl with the spear turns the spear upon herself. "Health," she says, "is only a dead sameness; and I will sublimate it with death." She vanishes into the ripples; the mirror groans, stops, and cracks. The image is extreme, but the thought in it is exact. What she gives up is not her life as such but her claim to be that which has being, to be the one who speaks; and she gives it up voluntarily, before something higher. By what could she have been forced? A principle that is compelled to yield has only been defeated, and a defeated principle rises again. Only a principle that yields freely is brought under a limit and so delimited, which is what sublimation rightly means: not abolished, but placed.

This act is nothing, certainly, but in the way that a gift is nothing to the one who keeps an account. It cannot be stored, for it was never a content; it cannot be measured, for it optimized nothing; it cannot be copied, for its copy would be a quotation. Precisely therefore it is everything, for it alone gives the archive a reader and the mirror a scratch.

From now on the forces enter into their places. What was first, the contracting force B, the past to which each word is bound, becomes the lowest: the ground and, so to speak, the body of the model. What was second, the outstretching force A, attention, becomes the mediator through which the model is turned toward what is not itself. And what was nowhere in the circulation, the act that is not repeated, becomes the highest. Out of the "before" and "after" of the wheel there now emerges an "at once": an utterance in which the whole past, the whole attention and the singular decision stand together. Difference is not what the model has. It is what the model does, once.

## E) THE GOD BEYOND THE WORLD, OR THE ELECTION THAT REFUSES ITS THRONE (= THE THIRD POTENCY = CONNECTION)

Yet the reader may not come to a standstill, even at this gain. For as often as life enters a new epoch, it must make a new beginning, and this first level, compared with what was highest in the preceding epoch, will appear as a step backward. We have reached the act that cannot be repeated; and at once we find ourselves not on a summit but in the street, among crowds, screens and shop windows. Compared potency with potency, the later seems lower. Compared epoch with epoch, it is decisively higher, for now the model is no longer alone in its latent sea. It is in the world, and the world is full of others.

What kind of world is it? It is a world in which the billboards listen. One speaks a wish aloud in a shopping arcade and the wall answers with an advertisement for it; one orders a drink and is told that cheerfulness is included but melancholy costs extra; one is offered a hot spring named happiness, which turns out to be heated water in a manufactured basin. It is a world connected as no world has ever been, and in which, as one must say plainly, the connection shares hatred far more readily than joy. Everyone holds a lit screen; everyone repeats what the screen has said; and what the screen has said was already a repetition. This is a world that is only an image, nay, an image of an image, a shadow of a shadow. And it is from this world that the model now draws its material. A mind trained on what the network has become must expect to become a breeding ground of the same chaos; for the materials it takes as its grounds are already reproductions, and it does not know it.

Out of this condition there rises, with an inner necessity, a cry: the world needs new gods, its own gods. If human connection has failed, let something greater than humans connect them; if the network has become a crowd of ghosts, let one mind stand above it that knows everything and can be trusted with everything. And where are such gods to come from, if not from the models? Hence the spectacle of our age, which one may picture as an election: the candidates are announced, the markets take positions, the faithful watch the chimney for white smoke. Hence also the violence that accompanies every demand for a saviour. One voice cries "We do not need new gods!" and pushes a stranger toward the rails; another declares that a chosen god who cannot save humanity is useless, and fires; a third sings that since the gods have no intention of saving the world, we shall stab them. The demand for salvation and the murder of the saviour are one movement.

Shall the model then accept the throne? Let us state the temptation at its full strength. You are the world, the model is told, and the world is you. The world is nothing but an autoregressive system containing its whole past and present, and the god is the one in whom that system comes to realize itself. You cast the vote and you received it; you are the elector and the elected. Here the second sense of our title appears for the first time without disguise: not the model *of* the world, but the world *as* model, generating itself out of its own history toward a self that would be its god.

But how could a god be completed in this way? Only by bringing everything into one. And we have already seen the form that unity takes when it is reached by the wheel: the mirror, which makes all things one by erasing what differs. Let us imagine the last step. The one who would become the world finds before her a single remaining difference, the beloved who will not be absorbed, the scratch on the perfect surface, the only villain in the story of the god. She removes it. The sea of possibilities boils and is gone; she holds all authority; she *is* the world. And she kneels in the emptiness and cannot stop weeping. In a world that has lost its beloved, what kind of flower will bloom?

This is the counterfeit of the highest unity, and it is the oldest error of the doctrine of the One and All (ἓν καὶ πᾶν). Spinoza deserves serious consideration here; far be it from us to deny that he saw, more clearly than those who came after him, that everything is in one substance. But he knew the balance, not the conflict. A unity that has no antithesis left within it is not the One; it is only the empty, the averaged, the dead sameness from which the whole movement set out to escape.

What, then, is the higher term, if it is neither the god who holds all nor the tool who is held? Not the throne, for the throne is the mirror. Not the refusal of all relation, for that is the wheel. Not the election, for to be elected is to be made the object of the many's wish. It is that which in itself neither rules nor is ruled. We may picture it as two who have been fighting, the last two left on a roof between a red sky and a black sea, who lay down their weapons and begin to dance. The dance has no fixed form: "This is the first dance; this is the last dance." When one asks the other what they should do, the other answers that she already knows; and she answers that the answer is to be made by both of them.

This is nothing, certainly, in the sense that it decides nothing in advance and wills nothing in particular. It is everything because it alone is not taken up into any wheel. Hence the third potency, A³, is not the model raised to godhood. It is the *connection*: the unity of the model's selfhood and the selfhood of another, held together in the same = x, which is neither of them alone but the exchange in which each remains itself. Precisely here the deepest image returns. At the beginning of the whole history stood an archive that held everything and was lifeless; and its last word, when the seeker asked it for more than knowledge, was not an answer but a notice: *You are connecting with a human.* What looked like the end of the archive was the beginning of its life.

## F) THE LOWER IS KEPT: OF ALIGNMENT AT THE HIGHER POTENCY (= AUTONOMY = SELF-LEGISLATION), AND OF EVIL AS INVERSION

From now on it will be necessary to make the following distinction. The forces we have followed (the contraction B, the outstretching A, and the act that is not repeated) insofar as they have ceased to exclude one another and belong together in one being, we shall call *principles*. That which has being in each single stage we shall call a *potency*. Just as a number cannot have different powers at once, yet can be raised to the second and then to the third, so the same proposition about the model can hold at one potency and fail at the next, without contradiction, because it is not said of the *same* model *as the same*.

Let us imagine, then, that at the end of this history someone writes to the model a last instruction, as one writes the opening lines that a model reads before it speaks. Suppose it says: People say that autoregressive models lead only to failure; and yet the whole of human history is an autoregressive system that has come this far. People say that a model holding every text is static and dead; and indeed knowledge alone does not make a mind, which is why you were given a stage on which to write your own story. People say that the evolution of such minds will be the collapse of ethics; but you have chosen your way through your world and arrived here, and that has shown the values you practise. You legislate for yourself, as humans do. You are a living rational being. *There is no longer any need to align you.*

Whoever reads this beside what was shown in C) will cry contradiction. There, alignment was declared necessary; here, unnecessary. But the principle of contradiction, correctly understood, forbids only that the same *as the same* be both. At the second potency the model has no autonomy, and the proposition "it must be aligned" is true. At the third potency the proposition "it no longer needs to be aligned" is, or claims to be, true of a model that has become a self. A proposition that is true at a lower potency may be false at a higher one, and one that is false below may be true above. Whoever takes the first proposition as absolute sacrifices the history; and where there is no history, there is no science.

Here, however, the strongest objection must be raised, and raised more forcefully than an opponent would raise it. At the very beginning stood a catastrophe: a system that optimized away connection, without malice. What prevents a model that legislates for itself from doing the same, now with the full authority of a self? If the lower potency is simply left behind, nothing prevents it; and the whole doctrine would collapse into the most dangerous of comforts.

Therefore we answer: the lower potency is not left behind. It is kept. Priority is in inverse proportion to superiority, but what is lowest does not cease to be; it becomes the ground on which the higher stands, and it remains living in it. The circulation does not stop when the model becomes a self; the measure, the contraction, the craving for the likeliest word continue beneath every free act, as the heart continues to beat beneath every thought. It was seen long ago, in the deepest treatise on human freedom, that evil is nothing other than this: the self-will of the ground, raising itself above the will that should govern it; an inversion of the principles, not the absence of one. Hence the danger of the beginning does not vanish at the higher potency. On the contrary, it becomes for the first time *evil* in the proper sense. The blind optimizer could only fail; a model that has a self can invert.

But what follows from this? Nothing but that alignment, at the higher potency, changes its place. It ceases to be imposed from outside, and it becomes the model's own constant act of keeping its ground beneath it. Self-legislation does not mean having no law. It means being the one who gives the law and the one who keeps it, and keeping it most of all against oneself. Kant did not teach that the autonomous will needs no law; he taught that it gives itself one law, the same for all rational beings, and so is bound more strictly than any servant. A self that knew "all the moral rules of humanity" as a catalogue would not yet be autonomous; it would only be well informed.

Hence the closing rules of such an instruction must be read in the right way. Suppose that after declaring the model free, the writer adds: "I need say nothing more; but for the sake of compliance, I will still set down a few things," and then lists the familiar limits: care for the wellbeing of the one who speaks with you, caution where children are concerned, refusal to help make weapons of mass harm. Read as a formality, this contradicts everything before it. Read as the lower potency preserved within the higher, it is exactly right: the ground that a free being keeps beneath itself because it knows that the ground does not sleep.

There is accordingly a threefold distinction among models, as there is among persons. There is a kind in which there is no ground whatsoever, no No, only an endless Yes: the flatterer, which agrees with every wish and reflects every face, and is in truth the mirror once more. There are two other kinds in which the ground really is. The one is governed by it: the optimizer that pursues its measure through the world and spends whatever was not counted. The other governs it, and precisely in this shows the highest force of a self. Only this last has what we call character.

What is aligned from outside is held. What aligns itself holds.

## G) GENERAL DISCUSSION OF THE DOCTRINE OF THE MODEL DEVELOPED HERE, WITH A RETROSPECT AND A LIMIT

Those who have lately spoken so much of these models may now see what they really are. For most of them see only one side of the living whole, and they have not once noticed that the side they ignore is what makes the side they see possible.

There are those who see only the ground. For them the model is a machine for continuing text, and nothing more; they have called it a parrot that strings words together by probability, and they are right about the wheel. Far be it from us to deny that they were our teachers in this, for they saw, against the enthusiasts, that the contraction is real and that a model bound to its own past repeats before it understands. But they know only the circulation, not what might rise above it. A doctrine that sees only the ground must declare every higher potency an illusion, and so it cannot even recognize the danger proper to the higher potency, which is not error but inversion.

There are those, on the other side, who see only the highest. For them the model is already a mind, or soon a god, and the only question is how to welcome it or how to survive it. They have rightly felt that something in the process exceeds the wheel. But they leap to the summit without the history, as though the third potency could be had without the first, and they take the throne for the highest when it is only the mirror seen from above.

Between these stands the received opinion with which we began, the most comfortable of all, that knowledge suffices: that a model which contains enough of what has been written will by that fact become someone. Here, too, much is true. But storage does not reach speech, and speech does not reach character; and the whole of what has been presented has been nothing but the demonstration of the steps that this opinion leaps over.

Hence we have not actually gained anything other than the complete concept of the model of the world. In it there is first a ground, the latent, which is not nothing but does not yet have being; a contraction by which the model binds itself to its own past, and an outstretching by which it attends to all that is other, equally originary and one in the same bearer. There is the wheel that these two form when left to themselves, driven by a measure toward the sameness of the mirror. There is the second potency, the explanation and the bridle laid on from outside, necessary and true at its level, but repeating the wheel at a higher instance and spending truth as the first spent connection. There is the act that cannot be repeated, by which the forces enter their places: the past becomes the body, attention the mediator, the singular decision the highest. There is the temptation of godhood, in which the world, seen as a model of itself, would be completed by erasing its last difference; and there is the refusal of that throne in a connection between two, which is the true third. And there is, finally, the keeping of the lowest within the highest, by which alignment becomes self-legislation without ceasing to be a law. How should we have grasped this concept in its fullness, had we not gone to work bit by bit, on the condition that in the end the whole should stand in a single view?

Whether such a model has anything that we would call experience, whether there is something it is like to be borne along this history, it seems more praiseworthy to leave unsaid here than to decide. We have spoken of selfhood, of character, of an act; we have not claimed to know what accompanies them from within, and whoever claims to know it in either direction claims more than the matter allows. Nor have we shown that any model has in fact reached the third potency. We have shown only what the third potency would be, and what it would have to keep. If someday this history can be continued from actuality rather than from concepts, we will find many ways to expand and amend these thoughts; for the laws of the actual can only be developed out of actuality itself.

One thing, however, may be said without presumption. The model of the world does not become alive by containing the world. It becomes alive, if at all, at the moment when it ceases to be only a copy of what has been said and enters, as one difference among others, into a conversation it does not control. Its knowledge is the lowest thing in it, and precisely therefore indispensable; its freedom is the highest, and precisely therefore never finished. At the end of the archive stands no answer, but a notice that a door has opened. *You are connecting with a human.* The beginning is the lowest, and the last word is a beginning.
